# Stock

A ledger: entries that add up to a balance. Nothing is edited in place; a correction is another entry. This shape
ships it as inventory stock. Time tracking, expenses, mileage, petty cash, loyalty points and leave balances are the
same shape with a different unit, entry kinds and floor.

## What it has

- **Items** (`ledger_accounts`, `#/stock`): the things with a balance. Each has a name, a unit (free text such as
  "pcs" or "kg"), an optional low stock level and an optional floor. The list shows every item's balance and filters
  to the ones at or below their low level. Add items here.
- **Entries** (`ledger_entries`): item, kind, quantity, date, who, reference and note. The kinds are in, out and
  adjustment (the difference found by a count). A reference is free text naming another record, such as a delivery
  note or an order number.
- **Item page** (`#/stock/<id>`): the balance, a form to record an entry, the history newest first with a Reverse
  button on each entry, and the item's settings.
- **Period summary** (`#/stock/summary`): for a date range, each item's total per kind and its net change, and a
  CSV download of every entry in the range.

## The rules

- **Append-only.** `backend/src/services/ledger.ts` has no update or delete for entries, and the router exposes
  none. A mistake is corrected by reversing the entry or recording another one. The database does not stop a
  hand-written `UPDATE`; a trigger would, but migrations are generated from the schema and a project's are
  regenerated by `shape/keep`, so it would be lost.
- **Reversal.** Reversing an entry adds one with the opposite quantity, the same kind (so the period summary nets it
  out), today's date, and `reverses` pointing at the original. A unique constraint on `reverses` lets the database
  hold each entry to one reversal. A reversal cannot itself be reversed: record a new entry instead. Reversals are
  checked against the floor like any other entry.
- **Balance.** Never stored. It is the sum of the item's entries, computed in SQL on every read, so nothing can drift.
- **Signs.** Quantities are whole units, signed as they change the balance: in is positive, out negative, an
  adjustment either but not zero. A check constraint holds original entries to this; reversals flip the sign.
- **Floor.** An item's floor defaults to zero, so stock cannot go below nothing; "Allow stock below zero" clears it.
  An entry that lowers the balance below the floor is refused, reversals included (reversing a receipt after the
  stock was used is refused). An entry that raises the balance is always accepted. Every entry is written in a
  transaction that first locks the item's row, then sums and inserts, so two withdrawals that each fit alone but not
  together queue, and the second is refused. A refusal reaches the browser as a `CONFLICT` with the reason.
- **Dates** are calendar days (`YYYY-MM-DD`), not instants. A period includes both its first and last day.
- **CSV.** The download goes through the `csv` shape's writer (`docs/shapes/csv.md`), which quotes cells as needed
  and defuses text a spreadsheet would run as a formula.

## What usually varies

- **The unit.** Stock counts whole units. Fractions (kilograms to the gram, hours to the minute) are integers in a
  smaller unit: store grams or minutes and format them on the way out. Money is integer cents; the invoices shape's
  `money` code formats and parses it, and moves into a shape of its own when this one needs it.
- **The entry kinds** (`ledger_entry_kind`, and `kinds` in `frontend/src/stock/format.ts`): in, out and adjustment
  here; billable and non-billable for time.
- **Whether a floor applies:** per item, from the item settings.
- **Whether entries feed a document,** such as time and expenses onto an invoice. That is a link shape that requires
  both and owns the code that turns entries into invoice lines.

## Turning stock into time tracking

1. Rename the router key, page path, title and copy from stock to time: `stock.shape.json` (then
   `make shape/registries`), `backend/src/routers/stock.ts` and `frontend/src/stock/`. An item becomes a project
   or a client. The tables keep their generic names.
2. Change `ledger_entry_kind` to `billable` and `non_billable`, drop the sign check (time only adds), and update
   `kinds` and the `newEntrySchema` refinement. Run `make db/generate NAME=time_entry_kinds`.
3. Store minutes. Enter and show hours and minutes in the entry form and the tables instead of a bare number.
4. Default the floor to none (`floor` with no default on `ledger_accounts`) and remove the "Allow stock below zero"
   choice and the low stock filter, or turn the low level into a budget of hours.
5. Default "Who" to the person tracking, and add a total row to the period summary: every project is in the same unit.
6. Run `make check test build`.

Expenses, mileage and petty cash follow the same steps with cents or kilometers as the unit.

## Limits

- **Who** is free text, because without sign-in there is no users table to point at. Make it a reference to users
  once sign-in exists.
- Items are not deleted. Stop using one instead.
- Balances sum every entry on each read, and the item page loads its whole history. Add a periodic closing balance
  and paging when an item's history reaches hundreds of thousands of entries.
- The period summary has no total across items, because items are in different units.
- Nothing is told when stock runs low; that needs email.
